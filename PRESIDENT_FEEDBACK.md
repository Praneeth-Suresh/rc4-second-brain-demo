# CSC President Feedback — RC4 Second Brain

## Approval Decision

**Conditional approval: proceed with the fictional, offline demo and NUS AI Know discovery only. This is not approval to ingest RC4 documents, enable real users, or launch a pilot.**

The proposal identifies a real handover and retrieval problem, and the demo PRD is unusually disciplined about not overstating security or platform readiness. Before CSC can approve a controlled pilot, the must-have conditions below need named owners and written evidence.

## Overall Assessment

| Review area | Assessment | President view |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decision usefulness | Promising but not yet measurable | The demo shows retrieval, but the approval case needs a small set of priority questions, a baseline for current effort, and a pass threshold that would justify continuing. |
| Governance clarity | Directionally strong, operationally incomplete | Roles and controls are named, but accountable decision-makers, approvals, service levels, and incident authority are still unresolved. |
| Stakeholder trust | Strong demo honesty; real-pilot trust remains unproven | The persistent demo disclosure, fictional data, citations, freshness states, and refusal states are the right foundation. Trust will fail if simulated permission or audit controls are mistaken for enforcement. |
| Demo credibility | Credible as approval evidence, not as platform evidence | A deterministic offline walkthrough can prove the intended user experience and safety messaging. It cannot prove NUS AI Know suitability, access control, auditability, corpus quality, or operational adoption. |

## Must-Have Changes Before Pilot Approval

1. **Separate the decisions and name their owners.**
   Record who can approve (a) the demo presentation, (b) platform suitability, (c) each corpus category, (d) pilot users and access groups, and (e) expansion or shutdown. Replace “to be confirmed” sponsor and generic review-group language with a named role, accountable person or office, decision date, and escalation route.

2. **Obtain written NUS/platform confirmation before any ingestion.**
   The approval pack must answer the six unresolved questions in the proposal and PRD: permitted document categories; storage and retrieval boundary; NUS identity/group restriction; auditability; restrictions on committee, finance, resident, and personal data; and logging/model-training behaviour. A platform demonstration is not a substitute for this confirmation.

3. **Define the pilot corpus as an allowlist with an owner sign-off record.**
   Start with a small, enumerated set of document types and exclusions, rather than broad labels such as “approved committee process notes.” Every item needs a document owner’s written permission, classification, permission group, review date, retention/removal instruction, and source-of-truth location. No personal, welfare, disciplinary, confidential finance, or informal-message corpus should enter by default.

4. **Create a measurable decision-usefulness evaluation.**
   Specify 5–8 representative leadership tasks, the expected source-backed answer, review criteria, a current baseline (for example, time to find reliable context), pilot success thresholds, and failure thresholds. Capture when the system gives no answer, cites stale material, misses decisive context, or creates a misleading draft. Do not proceed to wider access based only on a positive walkthrough.

5. **Make accountability and incident handling executable.**
   Define the technical maintainer’s authority, document owner obligations, review cadence, removal SLA, how removals propagate to answers, the data-concern reporting channel, incident lead, and who can pause access. “Clear removal process” and “escalation path” must become documented operating procedures before real data is introduced.

6. **Set explicit pilot stop conditions.**
   Suspend ingestion or access on any unauthorized corpus inclusion, inability to confirm platform boundaries, incorrect permission exposure, uncitable or materially misleading answers, failure to remove withdrawn content, or absence of an accountable sponsor. State who has authority to stop the pilot and how affected users are notified.

## Should-Have Changes Before Stakeholder Presentation

1. **Lead the demo with one high-value decision workflow.**
   Show a leader preparing a recurring event or handover task, then quantify the decision it improves: what was decided, what evidence supports it, what remains uncertain, and who must approve the next action. Avoid presenting broad “ask anything” capability as the main value proposition.

2. **Add a visible “what this cannot prove” panel.**
   Alongside existing demo disclosures, explicitly state that the demo does not validate retrieval quality on real documents, NUS security controls, group authorization, audit records, retention enforcement, model-training settings, or user adoption.

3. **Show negative and degraded cases in the canonical walkthrough.**
   Include a stale source, an unsupported question, a prohibited-data refusal, and a withdrawn-source exclusion. These scenarios build more approval confidence than a sequence of only successful answers.

4. **Tighten language that may create false confidence.**
   In the proposal, “secure” and “reliable” should be framed as intended pilot properties pending platform confirmation and evaluation. In the demo, every simulated permission group, confidence/coverage state, and governance status should explain its basis and its non-enforcement status.

5. **Prepare a stakeholder and adoption plan.**
   List contributing committees, document owners, likely pilot users, faculty/administrative sponsor, NUS contacts, and communication expectations. Adoption should not depend on CSC Tech informally becoming the owner of every committee’s operational record.

## Approval-Meeting Questions That Need Answers

1. Which one or two recurring leadership decisions will the pilot improve first, and what measurable result would show that it helped?
2. Who has final authority to permit a document category and to halt access when a concern is raised?
3. Which NUS office will give written confirmation of storage, access, audit, logging, and model-training boundaries?
4. What is the removal and correction process, including the maximum time for a withdrawn document to stop influencing answers?
5. What evidence will distinguish “a polished demo” from “a safe, useful controlled pilot” at the next approval gate?

## Conditions for the Next Approval Gate

CSC should consider a controlled pilot only after it receives: written NUS/platform answers; a sponsor and RACI-style decision record; a signed, itemized corpus allowlist; an operating procedure for review, removal, and incidents; pilot metrics and stop criteria; and demo evidence that clearly shows citations, stale/withdrawn handling, unsupported queries, and sensitive-data refusals.

Until then, the appropriate message to stakeholders is: **the concept and demo are approved for evaluation, while real-data use and pilot access remain explicitly unapproved.**
